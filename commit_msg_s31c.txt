fix(study31): the weekly review counts the rubber-stamp, not just the approval

Study 31, third batch: G31-04.

The review's approval line could read a night of timeouts as vigilance: `answer_rate` counts a
question as answered when anybody wrote a verdict, and the timeout refused by the system sits in
the same `refused`-shaped silence as a person's no. The habituation the literature measures
(2606.22721: approval rises with reviewer exposure while inline comments fall; the fast-yes is the
signature) was invisible — and the columns that would show it did not exist.

`summarize_answers` gains the columns, computed once where the review and `chimera approve` both
read: `person_answered`/`person_approved`/`person_refused` (rows whose `approver_kind` is
`person` — the column G31-01 added; a row without one is read as a person, because every row
written before G31-01 was a person's), and `fast_approvals`/`fast_approval_rate` — a person's yes
given in under `FAST_SECONDS` (10 s, generous: the 200-character excerpt alone rarely fits in
less). The rate is None until at least one person answered, the same rule as every rate here.

The weekly review carries the line, only when a person answered at least one question: a week of
pure timeouts gets the overall line and nothing more — a 0% fast-approval rate over zero person
answers would read as a measured vigilance. The threshold is named in the text, so the number is
readable without the source.

tests/test_how_every_question_ended_is_remembered.py: the columns count the person answers only.
tests/test_the_weekly_review_counts_what_the_screens_count.py: the review's line, the silence over
zero person answers, and the overall totals unchanged.
